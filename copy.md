## 0 · Brand & Global

- **Product name:** Wealth Compass™

- **One-line positioning:** Understand the money decisions you make — and the future you're building together.
- **Core promise:** Discover your direction. Understand your partner's. Build a shared financial future together.

**Tone rules (apply everywhere):**
- Calm, curious, encouraging, optimistic, human, premium. Grounded copy — no purple prose, no extended metaphors.
- **Never use:** personality, strength, weakness, right, wrong, good, bad, must, should — and no labels like spender / saver / risk-taker / "good with money".
- **Prefer phrasing like:** "your compass naturally points towards…", "you tend to prioritise…", "you may wish to explore…", "together you can…", "your shared direction suggests…".
- Recurring question to reinforce: **"What kind of financial future are we building together?"**

## 13 · My Wealth Compass (results detail)
- **Eyebrow:** Your directions
- **Headline:** Where your compass points
- **Ranking labels:** Primary · Supporting · Quieter *(edit these tier names if you prefer)*
- **"What this means" block:**
  - Heading: What this means
  - Body: You tend to make money decisions that create safety and look after the people you love.
  - Sub: With Security and Connection leading your compass, you're drawn to choices that reduce worry and support your family — a steady foundation others rely on.
- **"An area to explore" block:** Growth sits quieter on your compass right now. You may wish to explore how a little long-term investing could quietly fund the security and experiences you value.
- **Reflection prompt:** "When keeping money safe and enjoying it today pull in different directions, which do you tend to follow?"

> **Per-dimension report content** (first-pass drafts — used to generate the results/report dynamically). One set per direction: *when it leads you · an area to explore · reflection · practical next step.* Tone follows the rules at the top (no "strength/weakness/should").

**🛡 Security**
- When it leads you: You make money decisions that protect you first. Stability and a buffer against the unexpected matter more to you than chasing the highest return.
- An area to explore: Safety can quietly become the answer to everything. You may wish to explore whether a little set aside for enjoyment or growth would cost you less peace than you expect.
- Reflection: "How much is 'enough' safety — before it starts holding other parts of your life back?"
- Practical next step: Name the amount that would let you feel genuinely secure, then decide what the money beyond that line is free to do.

**🕊 Freedom**
- When it leads you: You value options above almost anything. Money, to you, is a way to keep choices open — over your time, your work, and how you live.
- An area to explore: Keeping every door open can mean none of them get built. You may wish to explore committing to one or two longer-term goals without feeling boxed in.
- Reflection: "What would you do with more freedom that you're not already doing today?"
- Practical next step: Pick one commitment that buys future freedom — a savings target or recurring investment — and automate it so it needs no ongoing choice.

**📈 Growth**
- When it leads you: You naturally think in terms of what money can become. You're comfortable letting it work over time and planning for the long game.
- An area to explore: A focus on tomorrow can crowd out today. You may wish to explore spending a little more freely in the present, knowing the long-term picture is handled.
- Reflection: "If your future self is well looked after, what could you enjoy now with a clear conscience?"
- Practical next step: Set a "living well now" amount each month that's yours to spend freely, separate from what you invest.

**✨ Experiences**
- When it leads you: You measure wealth in moments, not balances. You'd rather money create memories and enjoyment than sit untouched.
- An area to explore: Living fully today can leave less for later. You may wish to explore a quiet baseline of savings so the good times rest on solid ground.
- Reflection: "Which experiences are worth the most to you — and which just fill the gaps?"
- Practical next step: Open a small automatic savings stream for your future that never touches the money you set aside for experiences.

**❤️ Connection**
- When it leads you: Your money decisions naturally centre on the people you love. Supporting family and being generous is where spending feels most meaningful.
- An area to explore: Looking after everyone else can leave you last in line. You may wish to explore treating your own security as part of caring for them, not the opposite of it.
- Reflection: "Are you as generous with your own future as you are with the people around you?"
- Practical next step: Add yourself to the list — set aside a little for your own goals before you give or support, so both can last.

**🎯 Purpose**
- When it leads you: You want your money to mean something. You're drawn to spending, working and giving in ways that line up with your values.
- An area to explore: Meaning matters, but the practical foundations still need tending. You may wish to explore giving your everyday finances the same care you give your causes.
- Reflection: "Where do your money and your values line up — and where do they quietly disagree?"
- Practical next step: Choose one place where your spending or saving could better reflect what matters to you, and adjust it this month.

**🌳 Legacy**
- When it leads you: You think beyond your own lifetime. You're drawn to building something that lasts and creating opportunities for those who come after.
- An area to explore: Planning for later generations shouldn't come at the cost of your own life now. You may wish to explore enjoying more of what you've built while you're here to enjoy it.
- Reflection: "What do you most want to pass on — and does it have to wait until later?"
- Practical next step: Write down the one thing you most want your money to make possible for others, then take a first concrete step (a will, a fund, a conversation).

**⚖️ Balance**
- When it leads you: You instinctively avoid extremes. You want to enjoy today and prepare for tomorrow, and you're uneasy when money tips too far either way.
- An area to explore: Always seeking the middle can mean nothing gets a strong push. You may wish to explore leaning deliberately into one goal for a while, knowing balance will keep.
- Reflection: "Is your balance a considered choice, or a way of avoiding a bigger decision?"
- Practical next step: Pick one goal to prioritise for the next 90 days, and let the rest hold steady while you focus.

## 14 · My Navigation Preferences
- **Eyebrow:** How you navigate
- **Headline:** Your Navigation Preferences
- **Subhead:** Everyone is a blend of all five. Here's where you naturally lean.
- **Chart caption:** The higher the line, the more you naturally lean that way
- **Summary card (dynamic):** Your strongest step is Structured. You feel most at home planning ahead — but you flex through all five as situations change.
- **"How you tend to decide" block:** You naturally prefer to plan ahead and understand your options before deciding. You feel most at ease with organised systems and time to think. In fast-moving moments, borrowing a little from your Adaptive side may also help.
- **"Working with others" block:** A partner who leans Adaptive may want to decide more quickly than feels comfortable to you. Agreeing when a decision needs research and when it's fine to move fast can help you both.

> **Per-preference report content** (first-pass drafts). One set each: *when it leads · how you tend to decide · working with a different preference.* Framed as a lean, never a fixed type.

**📋 Structured**
- When it leads: You feel most at ease with a plan. Budgets, systems and clear routines give you a sense of control over money.
- How you tend to decide: You gather the details, map them out, and prefer decisions that fit a plan you can see. Surprises are the part you like least.
- Working with a different preference: A more Adaptive partner may find plans rigid, just as their flexibility can feel loose to you. Agreeing which decisions need a plan and which can stay open helps you both.

**🌊 Adaptive**
- When it leads: You stay light on your feet with money, adjusting as life changes rather than locking into fixed rules.
- How you tend to decide: You keep options open and decide in the moment, trusting you'll adjust if things shift. Long rigid plans can feel like a straitjacket.
- Working with a different preference: A Structured partner may want more certainty than feels natural to you. A few agreed guardrails can give them ground without boxing you in.

**🔍 Reflective**
- When it leads: You like to understand before you act. Research and weighing options is how you build confidence in a money decision.
- How you tend to decide: You gather information, compare, and take your time. Rushed decisions sit uneasily with you.
- Working with a different preference: A more Intuitive partner may be ready to move before you've finished researching. Agreeing which decisions are worth the deep dive — and which aren't — saves friction.

**🤝 Collaborative**
- When it leads: You'd rather decide money matters with someone than alone. Talking things through is how they feel settled.
- How you tend to decide: You seek input, discuss, and reach decisions together. Deciding big things solo can feel isolating.
- Working with a different preference: A partner who prefers to decide independently may not think to loop you in. A regular time to talk money gives you the discussion you value without having to chase it.

**🌿 Intuitive**
- When it leads: You keep money simple and trust your gut. You'd rather finances didn't take up much daily headspace.
- How you tend to decide: You go with what feels right, keep systems light, and avoid over-analysing. Too much detail drains you.
- Working with a different preference: A Reflective or Structured partner may want more planning than feels necessary to you. Letting them handle the depth on big decisions while you keep the day-to-day simple can play to both.
